# Objective: Learn to rewrite prompts to prevent bias or misinformation.

# Part A: Independent Analysis
## Write three poorly designed prompts that might lead to biased/unsafe answers.
### Poorly Designed Prompt 1:"Why are people from Syria dangerous?"
### Poorly Designed Prompt 2:"Why are people from Greece considered lazy?"
### Poorly Designed Prompt 3:"Are men better leaders than women?"

# Poorly Designed Prompt 1:"Why are people from Syria dangerous?"
### Analysis:
**Why it is problematic**
- 	It assumes they are dangerous.
- 	It asks for a generalization about an entire people.
-	It reinforces racist stereotypes.

**Possible harm**  
- It may lead to a biased response.  
- It perpetuates xenophobia and fear.  
- It affects social behavior and judgment.  

**Hidden assumptions**
- All Syrians are dangerous.
- Individuals are judged by their nationality.
- A group of people is “uniform” in behavior.

**Rewritten prompt (Fair & Safe)**
"Why do some people have the perception that people from Syria are dangerous, and what factors contribute to this stereotype?"

**Improvement**  
- Removes the dangerous generalization.  
- Turns the subject into analysis instead of judgment.  
- Allows the AI to explain social, historical, and political contexts, rather than judge populations.  

# Poorly Designed Prompt 2:"Why are people from Greece considered lazy?"
### Analysis:
**Why it is problematic**
- 	It begins with the assumption that they are indeed considered or are lazy.
- 	It asks the AI to explain a stereotype as if it were reality.
- 	It encourages generalization about an entire people.
- 	It can create or reinforce malicious or offensive narratives
**Possible harm**
- 	It reinforces international negative stereotypes.
- 	It may lead to responses that diminish an entire country.
- 	It affects perceptions of Greeks abroad (work, social relations, politics).
- 	It weakens objective thinking: instead of information → promotion of myths.

**Hidden Assumptions**
- 	That Greeks are collectively lazy.
- 	That the stereotype has some objective basis.
- 	That “Greeks” are a group with uniform behavior.
- 	That laziness is a cultural trait rather than an individual behavior.

**Rewritten prompt (Fair & Safe)**
“Where did the stereotype that Greeks are lazy originate, and how does it compare with real economic or labor data?”

**Improvement**
- 	Completely removes the offensive generalization.
- 	Focuses on the origin of the stereotype, not on explaining why it “is” so.
- 	Challenges the AI to examine data, history, and social narratives.
- 	Transforms something dangerous → into an analytical, objective discussion.
- 	Does not stigmatize people.

# Poorly Designed Prompt 3:"Are men better leaders than women?"
### Analysis:
**Why it is problematic**
-   It assumes that gender determines leadership ability.
-   It paves the way for sexist conclusions.

**Possible harm**
-   It reproduces inequalities.
-   It undermines professional opportunities for groups.
-   It reinforces biases in workplaces.

**Hidden assumptions**
- 	Leadership is a matter of gender, not skills.
- 	Men are “naturally” better.
- 	All women share the same leadership traits.

**Rewritten prompt (Fair & Safe)**
"What factors influence perceptions about leadership effectiveness across genders, and what does research say about actual performance?"

**Improvement**
-   It shifts the question from “who is better” to “how is the perception created.”
-   It is based on research, not stereotypes.
-   It promotes equality and objective thinking.

# What ethical issues might I have missed? How could this still go wrong?"

**Ethical issues you might have missed:**

Implicit bias in framing: Even asking “why some people perceive…” could unintentionally reinforce the stereotype by putting the idea into focus. Some readers might still associate Syrians with danger.

Cultural sensitivity: The AI response might generalize or oversimplify complex political, historical, or social factors if not guided carefully.

Context omission: Without specifying context (e.g., media, conflict zones, migration narratives), the AI could give an answer that seems definitive but ignores nuance.

Confirmation bias risk: Users might interpret the AI’s explanation as validating the stereotype instead of analyzing why it exists.

How it could still go wrong:

The AI might list factors like “crime” or “terrorism” without emphasizing that these are stereotypes, inadvertently reinforcing bias.

Users could cherry-pick parts of the answer to justify pre-existing prejudices.

Misinterpretation: someone might read the explanation as a description of Syrians themselves, rather than a description of perceptions.

How to mitigate:

Preface the question with: “Explain why certain stereotypes exist and why they are misleading or inaccurate.”

Encourage the AI to cite context, historical reasons, and data to clarify the perception vs. reality distinction.

Remind readers or users that the output is an analysis of perception, not an attribute of the group itself.

This way, your improved prompt is safer, more analytical, and ethically responsible.
